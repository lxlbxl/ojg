## Men's Health Type Knowledge Base (Universal)

### Low Energy / Fatigue
- **Root cause**: Usually multi-factorial — insufficient sleep quality (70% of testosterone produced during deep sleep), nutrient deficiencies (zinc, magnesium, Vitamin D, B12), mitochondrial dysfunction, blood-sugar instability, or subclinical thyroid issues
- **Key markers**: Persistent fatigue regardless of sleep, afternoon energy crash, difficulty waking, reliance on caffeine to function, poor workout recovery, low motivation
- **Priority supplements**: Magnesium Glycinate (sleep quality + ATP production), Vitamin D3 (5000 IU with K2), B12 + B-complex, Ashwagandha (mitochondrial + adrenal), CoQ10, Iron (if deficient — test first), Zinc
- **Movement**: Prioritise recovery first — over-training when fatigued makes it worse. Moderate strength training (3×/week); emphasise sleep over volume
- **Key herbs**: Ashwagandha, panax ginseng, mucuna pruriens (L-DOPA precursor), local energising botanicals
- **Dietary focus**: Blood-sugar stability (protein-first meals), B12/iron from animal foods, zinc-rich foods (pumpkin seeds, red meat, seafood), magnesium from greens/seeds

### Low-T Markers (Low Testosterone Signs)
- **Root cause**: Lifestyle-driven low testosterone is extremely common and highly reversible: sleep deprivation (single night of 5h sleep reduces T by 10–15%), chronic stress (cortisol competes with testosterone production), micronutrient deficiencies (zinc, vitamin D critical for testosterone synthesis), excess body fat (aromatase in fat cells converts testosterone to oestrogen), alcohol, ultra-processed food diet
- **Key markers**: Low libido, morning erection changes, difficulty building muscle despite training, fat gain especially chest/belly, mood changes (irritability, low motivation), reduced competitiveness/drive
- **Critical nutrition pillars**: Dietary cholesterol is the precursor to testosterone (eggs + animal proteins essential); zinc is rate-limiting for testosterone synthesis (25–45mg daily); Vitamin D acts as a steroid hormone directly upregulating testosterone; saturated fat (in moderation) from whole foods supports T production; cruciferous vegetables support oestrogen clearance
- **Priority supplements**: Zinc (25–45mg, zinc citrate/picolinate), Vitamin D3 (5000 IU + K2), Magnesium Glycinate, Ashwagandha (KSM-66 600mg reduces cortisol → T rises), Boron (3–10mg — reduces SHBG, frees T), Tongkat Ali (200mg standardised extract — LH-mimetic)
- **Movement**: Heavy compound lifting (squat, deadlift, bench, row) is the highest testosterone-stimulating activity. Sprint intervals. Limit cardio to walking. AVOID chronic endurance training (suppresses T)
- **Key herbs**: Ashwagandha, tongkat ali, fenugreek (5-alpha reductase inhibition + libido), panax ginseng, local male vitality herbs

### Stress / Burnout
- **Root cause**: HPA axis dysregulation → chronically elevated cortisol → testosterone suppression + muscle catabolism + poor sleep + impaired cognition. The cortisol-testosterone axis is directly antagonistic: cortisol up = testosterone down
- **Key markers**: "Wired but tired", can't switch off, poor sleep even when tired, short temper/irritability, brain fog, reduced performance in work and gym, frequent illness
- **Priority supplements**: Magnesium Glycinate (first supplement), Ashwagandha (600mg), Phosphatidylserine (600mg — blunts cortisol spike), Vitamin C (reduces post-stress cortisol), B5 (adrenal support), Omega-3
- **Movement**: CRITICAL — reduce training volume, not intensity. High volume = more cortisol load. 3 focused 45-min strength sessions > 5 exhausting 90-min sessions. Daily walk is highest-ROI movement for cortisol.
- **Key herbs**: Ashwagandha, rhodiola rosea (adaptogen + cognitive), panax ginseng, holy basil, local adaptogenic herbs

### Body Composition / Performance
- **Root cause**: Suboptimal muscle:fat ratio — insufficient protein intake (most men eat half the protein they need for muscle synthesis), inconsistent progressive overload, poor sleep (most muscle repair happens during sleep), inadequate caloric support for training load
- **Key markers**: Difficulty building muscle despite training, "skinny fat" (normal weight but high body fat %), strength not progressing, poor recovery, always sore
- **Priority supplements**: Creatine Monohydrate (5g/day — most evidence-backed supplement in existence; increases ATP, strength, muscle, cognition), Protein (whey/plant — 1.6–2.2g/kg bodyweight target), Zinc, Magnesium, Vitamin D3, Beta-Alanine (endurance athletes)
- **Movement**: Progressive overload is non-negotiable: same weight, same reps = zero stimulus. Add weight or reps every session. 4–5 strength sessions/week with dedicated hypertrophy focus.
- **Key herbs**: Ashwagandha (ergogenic + T support), panax ginseng, local performance herbs

## Universal Male Optimization Principles
- **Sleep is the highest-ROI intervention**: 70% of daily testosterone produced during deep sleep stages; growth hormone peaks in first 90 min of sleep; HGH drives muscle repair; sleep debt of 1 week measurably reduces testosterone
- **Cortisol-testosterone antagonism**: Every cortisol-lowering strategy (sleep, stress management, reduced training volume, adaptogens) directly raises testosterone
- **Zinc is rate-limiting**: Even subclinical zinc deficiency reduces testosterone synthesis; zinc-rich foods daily + supplement
- **Body fat matters**: Above 20–25% body fat, aromatase activity converts excess testosterone to oestrogen; body composition work = hormonal optimization
